PRIMARY OBJECTIVE:

Implement a bowling alley and arcade environment that closely
matches the supplied concept art and reference screenshots.

The environment should feel like a real bowling alley from
approximately 1995–2005, recreated with the visual characteristics
of a high-quality PS2 game.

It should not look modern, futuristic, overly clean, abandoned,

It should feel like a functioning bowling alley that happens to

The nostalgic atmosphere, lighting, and sense of isolation are
more important than excessive geometric detail.

Use the provided concept art as the primary visual reference.

The descriptions below explain exactly how each model, material,
texture, and light source should behave.

============================================================
1. FUNDAMENTAL LIGHTING PHILOSOPHY
============================================================

CRITICAL:

Not everything that appears fluorescent or brightly coloured
should emit light.

Distinguish between five different material behaviours.

CATEGORY A — ACTUAL LIGHT EMITTERS

These objects produce visible light and contribute illumination
to nearby surfaces.

Examples:
- Fluorescent ceiling lights.
- Neon signs.
- Illuminated arcade marquees.
- Vending machine display illumination.
- Certain pinball machine bulbs.

These objects should have visible emissive materials and
corresponding direct or baked illumination.

CATEGORY B — ILLUMINATED SCREENS

These surfaces produce their own visible brightness and some
localized light spill.

Examples:
- Arcade CRT screens.
- Bowling score monitors.
- Small electronic displays.

Screens should exhibit controlled bloom and contribute subtle
coloured light to their immediate surroundings.

They must not illuminate an entire room.

CATEGORY C — UV-REACTIVE MATERIALS

These materials become vivid when exposed to blacklight
illumination but do not independently emit light.

Examples:
- Fluorescent cosmic carpet patterns.
- Certain painted wall decorations.
- Selected fluorescent bowling graphics.

Their appearance should depend on UV or simulated UV lighting.

They should not cast light, create unrealistic bloom, or
illuminate neighboring geometry.

CATEGORY D — REFLECTIVE MATERIALS

These materials reflect existing illumination.

Examples:
- Polished bowling lanes.
- Bowling balls.
- Vinyl chairs.
- Plastic drink bottles.
- Arcade cabinet glass.
- Metallic trims.

They may display bright reflections but do not emit light.

CATEGORY E — ORDINARY NON-EMISSIVE MATERIALS

Examples:
- Painted walls.
- Ceiling tiles.
- Table surfaces.
- Bowling pins.
- Cabinet bodies.
- Fabric and carpet backing.
- Most furniture.

They receive illumination normally.

Do not mistakenly give these objects emissive materials simply
because their textures contain bright colours.

============================================================
2. OVERALL ENVIRONMENT ATMOSPHERE
============================================================

The intended appearance is a combination of:

- A family bowling alley from approximately 2001.
- A small arcade attached to the bowling alley.
- Cosmic bowling nights with blacklights.
- Deep blue and purple ambient illumination.
- Occasional warm fluorescent lighting.
- Illuminated vending machines and arcade monitors.
- Colourful but slightly dated interior decorations.
- An unsettling absence of people.

The darkness should be intentional, not the result of broken
lighting or insufficient exposure.

The player must still be able to recognize all major props,
architecture, and surfaces.

The environment should contain strongly contrasting areas:

BRIGHT AREAS:
- Fluorescent ceiling fixtures.
- Bowling lane surfaces immediately under those fixtures.
- CRT monitors.
- Illuminated vending machines.
- Neon signage.
- Pinball playfields.

DARK AREAS:
- Unlit ceiling sections.
- Spaces between arcade cabinets.
- Upper corners of the room.
- Walls away from fixtures.
- Recesses below furniture.
- Empty areas around the arcade.

COLOURED AMBIENT AREAS:
- UV-reactive carpet.
- Blue light around vending machines.
- Colourful illumination around arcade screens.
- Soft violet or blue ambient fill.

The room should retain a predominantly dark blue appearance.

Avoid uniform ambient lighting.

There should be areas that feel bright and inviting, followed
by large regions of relatively dark space.

This uneven lighting is essential to the liminal atmosphere.

3. FLUORESCENT GALAXY CARPET
============================================================

MODEL:
Floor material applied to the carpeted areas.

GLOW:
UV REACTIVE.
NOT SELF-EMISSIVE.

This is one of the most important visual elements in the entire
environment.

DESCRIPTION:

The carpet should recreate the distinctive fluorescent carpeting
found in older bowling alleys, roller rinks, arcades, and cinemas.

The base material is extremely dark navy blue, almost black.

Across the surface are colourful cosmic patterns:

- Electric blue swirls.
- Violet nebula shapes.
- Fluorescent purple clouds.
- Magenta and pink highlights.
- Small orange and yellow planets.
- Shooting stars.
- Tiny white star clusters.
- Irregular fluorescent streaks.
- Abstract space-themed geometric shapes.
- Scattered brightly coloured particles.

The pattern should feel busy and chaotic without becoming
visually unreadable.

The dark navy background must occupy most of the surface.

The carpet must not appear as one continuous glowing texture.

LIGHTING BEHAVIOUR:

Under ordinary white lighting, the carpet should appear dark,
with the coloured graphics visible but relatively subdued.

Under blacklight illumination, the coloured patterns should
become significantly more saturated and visually striking.

The surrounding dark material should remain dark.

Fluorescent yellow, blue, pink, and orange details should
appear especially vivid.

IMPORTANT:

The carpet itself does not illuminate the room.

It should never cast blue, purple, or orange light onto props.

It should not have a general emissive value.

If Places does not support UV-reactive materials, simulate
the effect using localized UV-response shading or controlled
baked material variations.

Do not simply make the entire carpet emissive.

TECHNICAL TEXTURE REQUIREMENTS:

- Seamless repeating texture.
- 1024x1024 master texture.
- Appropriate runtime resolution for Places.
- Distinct shapes that survive downsampling.
- Correct mipmapping and texture filtering.
- Consistent UV scale across the environment.
- No obvious visible seams.
- No excessive texture repetition.
- No shimmering or aliasing during movement.

Avoid generating purely random colourful noise.

The carpet must contain actual recognizable cosmic motifs.

VISUAL TARGET:

Imagine walking into a bowling alley in 2002 with the lights
turned down for cosmic bowling.

The floor is almost black, but brightly coloured planets,
stars, and nebula patterns are visible everywhere.

That is the exact aesthetic being targeted.

5. FLUORESCENT CEILING LIGHT FIXTURES
============================================================

MODEL:
Recessed rectangular fluorescent light panels.

GLOW:
YES — STRONG ACTUAL LIGHT SOURCE.

DESCRIPTION:

Traditional rectangular fluorescent lighting panels recessed
into the suspended ceiling.

Use slightly translucent, textured plastic diffusers with
visible internal fluorescent tube patterns.

The fixture should resemble commercial lighting from the
1990s or early 2000s.

LIGHTING:

The illuminated surface should produce a bright neutral-white
or slightly warm-white glow.

Suggested appearance:
Approximately 4000–5000K fluorescent white.

Some fixtures may differ subtly in colour temperature.

Their illumination should:

- Brighten the floor directly underneath.
- Reveal the texture of nearby walls.
- Create soft illumination on seating.
- Produce strong highlights on polished bowling lanes.
- Illuminate nearby models consistently.
- Create believable indirect illumination.

Bloom should be restrained.

The light panel itself should appear intensely bright,
but it should not create enormous blurry white halos.

IMPORTANT:

Do not illuminate every fixture identically.

Some sections of ceiling can remain unlit to preserve the
darker overall atmosphere.

However, avoid excessive random flickering or broken lights.

This is an old bowling alley, not an abandoned horror level.

============================================================

- Bowling pins.

============================================================

This environment should convey a very specific feeling.

The player is exploring a familiar recreational space.

It appears operational.

The lights are on.

The arcade machines are running.

The vending machine is illuminated.

The bowling score monitors are active.

But nobody is there.

There are no customers.

There are no employees.

There is no visible reason for the building to be empty.

The emptiness itself creates the atmosphere.

DESIGN REQUIREMENTS:

- Preserve broad open floor areas.
- Use longer sightlines.
- Avoid excessive decorative clutter.
- Include slightly repetitive architectural details.
- Keep the space believable.
- Leave some corners relatively dark.
- Use warm and cold lighting contrasts.
- Avoid obvious horror decorations.
- Avoid heavy fog.
- Avoid excessive visual noise.
- Avoid exaggerated lighting effects.

The environment should be nostalgic first and unsettling
second.

It should evoke memories of childhood visits to bowling
alleys and arcades.

The unusual feeling comes from returning to those familiar
spaces and finding them completely deserted.

OPTIONAL AMBIENT AUDIO:

If appropriate systems already exist, consider:

- Subtle fluorescent fixture hum.
- Quiet vending machine refrigeration.
- Distant arcade attract-mode sounds.
- Gentle HVAC ambience.
- Occasional faint electronic noises.

Keep these sounds understated.

Do not require new audio systems merely to implement the
visual environment.

============================================================
28. LIGHTING IMPLEMENTATION FOR PLACES
============================================================

Apply the intended appearance through Places' existing
rendering, material, light-baking, and probe systems.

Use the established wgpu renderer.

Do not add unrelated rendering systems.

DIRECT LIGHTING:

Use actual direct lighting for sources that need to
illuminate surrounding objects.

Examples:

- Fluorescent fixtures.
- Neon signage.
- Vending machine lighting.
- Selected arcade illumination.

BAKED LIGHTING:

Bake static illumination wherever practical.

This is especially useful for:

- Ceiling lights.
- Signage.
- Arcade cabinet light spill.
- Large environmental colour contributions.

INDIRECT LIGHTING:

Ensure that static lighting contributes plausible indirect
illumination where supported.

The room should not have pitch-black surfaces immediately
beside bright lights.

MODEL LIGHTING:

Models must receive the same environmental illumination as
surrounding architecture.

A chair beneath a ceiling light must be visibly illuminated.

A bowling ball beside a vending machine should receive
appropriate nearby illumination.

Arcade cabinets should not become completely black outside
their emissive screens.

Avoid lighting discrepancies between models and static
environment geometry.

Do not allow model lighting to suddenly change based on
camera or player proximity.

This is especially important because Places has previously
experienced model-lighting artifacts.

Prevent:

- Black smudges.
- Incorrect shadow patches.
- Fully black models.
- Fully bright models in dark rooms.
- Proximity-dependent lighting changes.
- Excessive ambient occlusion.
- Incorrect probe interpolation.
- Light leaking through walls.
- Visible lighting discontinuities at geometry seams.

BLOOM:

Apply bloom only to genuinely bright emissive surfaces.

Examples:

- Neon tubing.
- Bright CRT displays.
- Fluorescent light panels.
- Selected arcade marquees.
- Vending machine illumination.

Ordinary saturated materials must not bloom simply because
they contain bright RGB values.

TONEMAPPING:

Preserve detail in bright sources.

The white fluorescent light panels should remain readable
rather than becoming enormous featureless white rectangles.

The dark navy walls should retain some colour information.

Avoid crushing the entire scene into black.

Avoid increasing global exposure so far that the liminal
darkness disappears.

COLOUR BLEEDING:

Use restrained local colour contributions.

The neon sign may tint the nearby wall red or blue.

An arcade screen may create a small blue reflection on its
control panel.

The vending machine may illuminate the nearby floor.

These effects should remain localized.

PERFORMANCE:

Prefer static and baked solutions for permanent lighting.

Use runtime lights selectively.

Keep models low-poly and optimize repeated assets.

Preserve existing Places performance expectations.

Do not sacrifice the visual style for unnecessary geometric
complexity.

============================================================
32. FINAL ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
============================================================

The environment is successful when:

1. It immediately resembles a real bowling alley and arcade
   from the late 1990s or early 2000s.

2. Its materials and models visually match the concept art.

3. The fluorescent carpet looks convincingly UV-reactive
   without behaving like a light source.

4. The arcade machines, score monitors, and vending machine
   produce localized illumination.

5. The bowling lanes appear warm and polished against the
   cooler dark blue environment.

6. Lighting clearly differentiates bright and dark areas.

7. Models receive correct direct and indirect illumination.

8. Emissive materials are used only where appropriate.

9. Bloom is restrained and does not wash out the environment.

10. The environment looks convincingly low-poly without
    appearing unfinished.

11. The layout feels like an actual recreational building.

12. The atmosphere communicates nostalgia, quiet isolation,
    and a subtle sense of unease.

13. The completed demo space demonstrates every major asset.

14. The implementation works within Places' existing engine
    architecture and quality settings.

15. The environment remains visually consistent when viewed
    from different positions and distances.

IMPORTANT FINAL ART-DIRECTION NOTE:

The goal is NOT a cyberpunk arcade.

The goal is NOT a futuristic neon entertainment center.

The goal is NOT an abandoned haunted bowling alley.

The goal is a perfectly ordinary bowling alley from 2001,
with fluorescent space carpet, old arcade machines, CRT
monitors, chunky furniture, painted bowling murals, and
dim cosmic-bowling illumination.

Everything should feel dated, familiar, colourful, and
strangely empty.

The scene should evoke the experience of being the last
person inside an old bowling alley at night.

That nostalgic, slightly dreamlike atmosphere is the
defining characteristic of the environment.

Use the supplied concept art as the primary visual target
throughout implementation.
