Beginner’s Guide to Your First Places Space

We’ll make a little world with three very different rooms: a quiet Backrooms office, a furnished Home room, and a tiled Pool room. Three linked elevator cabins will let you travel between them.

You don’t need to know the editor—or write any code—to follow along. We’ll start with a new project, build the rooms, try them in first person, and save a playable package.

Work through the steps in order. Each checkpoint describes what you should have before you continue. Click any picture to enlarge it; the red marks point to the controls mentioned in the text.

Workspace: the editor calls its basic workspace Simple. Stay in that workspace throughout this guide. You won’t need Precision controls or the [+] Advanced section.

This guide uses a mouse with a scroll wheel and a middle button. On a trackpad, a mouse makes the camera and drag tools much easier to use.

Open the editor

Keep the downloaded Places installation together, including its assets and compiler. The game and editor are separate executables.

InstallationEditor to open

On Windows: open the Places folder and double-click places-editor.exe.

On Mac: if you have Places.app, right-click it in Finder, choose Show Package Contents, and open Contents → MacOS. Open Terminal, drag places-editor from Finder into the Terminal window, and press Return. Use the same drag-and-Return method for the portable folder’s places-editor.

Double-clicking Places.app starts the game. There isn’t a separate editor app in this release package.

The screenshots and native walkthrough were recorded on Mac. The Windows executable name is verified against the packaging script; this guide does not claim a Windows hands-on test.

Create your project

  1. Click New at the upper left, or press ⌘N on Mac / Ctrl N on Windows.
  2. In the folder chooser, choose a parent folder where you want to keep your work. On Mac, confirm with Open. The editor makes a new untitled-… folder inside it.
  3. Keep track of that new folder. It contains the whole editable project; it is the folder you’ll select with Open next time.

The starter already gives you a square 6 × 6 m room, a floor, a ceiling and one fluorescent light. The ceiling is hidden when you look down from above, so you can see inside. It hasn’t been deleted.

You’ll add the walls yourself. The grey rectangle near the middle is the existing ceiling light.

Give it a useful name

  1. Click Project >, then Name.

Start here · 05 / 34

Get comfortable with the view

Try these before drawing anything:

  • Left-click the carpet to select the room. Its outline and handles appear. Click empty carpet rather than the grey light.
  • Press F, or click Frame, to bring the selection into view.
  • Hold the right mouse button and drag to orbit. Turn until you’re looking nearly straight down and the floor edges run roughly across and down the screen, like the pictures.
  • Hold the middle mouse button and drag to pan—slide your view without moving the room.
  • Scroll the wheel to zoom. Keep all four corners visible when drawing walls.

Leave Grid: on, Snap: on and Step 0.25m in the bottom bar. Click a toggle if necessary. The Step button cycles through sizes; keep clicking until it reads 0.25m.

The grid marks metres, while snapping lets your clicks and drags land in quarter-metre steps. A camera angle doesn’t need to match the screenshot perfectly. What matters is seeing the floor corners and the tool preview.

In the steps below, back means the far edge of the room in this overhead view; front means the near edge. If you lose your bearings, select the floor and press F again.

Backrooms · 06 / 34

Draw the outside walls

  1. Click the Build tab, then Wall.
  2. Click Chain: Off once so it reads Chain: On.
  3. Click the floor’s back-left corner. This sets the start.
  4. Move to the back-right corner. A wall preview appears. Check that it spans the room, then click to place it.
  5. Continue around the floor: click the front-right corner, then the front-left corner, then the original back-left corner.
  6. Press Esc to finish the chain.

That is five corner clicks for four walls. You’re drawing along the floor edges. The tool makes straight walls, 0.20 m thick, as tall as the room’s ceiling. Nearby endpoints snap together.

If a segment goes the wrong way, press Esc and use Undo or ⌘Z / Ctrl Z. Each completed wall is one undo step. Choose Wall again to redraw it.

Make a lobby and an open doorway

A short lobby gives the elevator some breathing room. We’ll divide the room, then cut a passage through the divider.

  1. With the same overhead view, choose Build → Wall. Leave Chain off for this single wall.
  2. On the left edge of the floor, find a point a little past halfway from back to front—about 3¾ m from the back. Click there, then click directly across on the right edge. The near lobby is about 2¼ m deep.
  3. Choose Doorway opening in the Build tab.
  4. Point at the middle of the new divider. Aim at its broad wall face, not empty floor. If the wall face is hard to see, right-drag to tilt your view a little.
  5. When the opening preview is centered, click once.

This creates an open passage without a door. Leave it that way in Backrooms; you’ll add a working door in Home.

Backrooms · 08 / 34

Add the first pillar

The rectangular pillar tool lives in the Tools menu. It uses a small form, even in Simple mode.

  1. Click Tools > → Geometry / stairs > → Rectangular pillar.
  2. Fill the first page as shown: X 1.5, Z 1.5, Width 0.5, Depth 0.5. Leave Base Y and Height blank; that makes the pillar run from the room’s floor to its ceiling.
  3. To change a field, click it, select all with ⌘A / Ctrl A, type the value, and press Return. Don’t type units.
  4. Click Preview. Check the pillar is inside the larger part of the room, then click Apply.

Keep the other pages at their defaults. The pillar uses yellow wallpaper. Here X and Z locate its bottom corner on the floor; these initial values give us an easy starting point for the copies.

Backrooms · 09 / 34

Make two copies and move them

  1. Keep the pillar selected and press ⌘D / Ctrl D. The copy appears slightly forward and to the right, and becomes selected. Wait until its colored handles are visible.
  2. Drag the copy’s red horizontal handle toward the right side of the office. Leave room to walk around it. Use its blue handle to line it up roughly with the first pillar.
  3. Duplicate that pillar once more. Again, use the new copy’s handles. If the handles haven’t caught up with the selection, click the new pillar once before dragging. Drag it left, then forward, so it sits below the first pillar in the picture.

Red moves across the room; blue moves along it. Grab the colored line rather than the pillar’s body. Release to keep the move. Esc cancels a drag, and Undo restores a move after you release.

The screenshot’s pillars sit roughly at the back left, back right and middle left. Their exact positions aren’t important; keep the central path and doorway clear.

Backrooms · 10 / 34

Choose a desk, then move it

  1. Click clear carpet to select the room and press F. Orbit back to the overhead view if needed.
  2. Click Assets → Props. Click the search field, type core:desk, and press Return.
  3. Click the Desk thumbnail, then Place chosen prop.
  4. The desk appears near the center of your current view. Drag its blue handle toward the back wall, stopping about a metre from that wall. Keep it between the two rear pillars.

Place chosen prop adds immediately; it doesn’t ask for another click on the floor. You can pan first to choose the general placement area, then use the handles to adjust it.

Before choosing a different prop, click the floor. Picking a catalog thumbnail while a prop is selected changes that existing prop into the new model.

Home · 12 / 34

Create Home’s floor and ceiling

  1. Click clear Backrooms carpet to select that room.
  2. Click Build → Room. The New Room form appears. A new room starts beside the selected room; there is no rectangle-drawing tool for this step.
  3. Change only West X to 10. Leave North Z 0, Width m 4, Depth m 6, Floor elevation m 0 and Room height m 2.7 as shown.
  4. Click Preview, then Apply. Press F to frame the new room.
  5. Find the yellow handle halfway along its right edge. Drag it outward until the preview says the room is 6.00 × 6.00 m, then release.

The single X value leaves a gap between the rooms for this elevator layout. You’re using the visual edge handle for the room’s size. If you release too early or go too far, drag the same handle again and watch the dimensions.

For now Home is only a floor and ceiling. It needs its own walls; the next step builds them.

Home · 13 / 34

Enclose Home and fit its door

  1. Keep Home in view from above. Choose Build → Wall and turn Chain on.
  2. Click Home’s floor corners in this order: back left → back right → front right → front left → back left. Press Esc when all four walls are placed.
  3. Choose Wall again, with Chain off. Draw a divider from left to right about 3¾ m from the back, just as in Backrooms.
  4. Choose Build → Door.
  5. Point at the middle of the divider’s wall face. Wait for a door preview, then click once.

The Door tool makes the opening and fits a working door in one action. You don’t need to cut a doorway first. It can also fit a door into an existing opening, but here we’re starting with a solid divider.

The door begins closed. You’ll open it with E when you test the room.

Home · 14 / 34

Give Home a wooden floor and painted walls

  1. Click an empty part of Home’s floor. Choose Assets → Surface, search for home:hardwood_oak_01, and click the oak thumbnail. The floor changes immediately.
  2. With the room still selected, click Material: … > in Properties. In the large catalog, click Surface once so the target reads Ceiling.
  3. Search for home:ceiling_white_01, then click its thumbnail. The catalog closes. You won’t see much change from above because the ceiling is hidden.
  4. Click a solid part of one wall, away from the door. Check that Properties says Wall. Open its Material: … > button, search for home:wall_paint_offwhite_01, and click the paint thumbnail.
  5. Repeat the wall-material step for the other three outside walls and the divider—five painted walls in total.

Opening Material from Properties starts a fresh surface selection. Use that button when changing from ceilings to walls; it avoids leaving the catalog aimed at the ceiling.

Lower a patch of floor to make the basin

The basin is a recessed part of the existing floor. It has its own bottom and vertical tiled edges; you don’t need to build four tiny walls around it.

  1. Click Tools > → Environment / space > → GLOBAL surface region.
  2. On the first page enter the values in the table. These place a 3 × 3 m basin toward the left side of the Pool room, leaving deck space around it.
  3. Click Page > for the remaining field and enter the edge material.
  4. Click Preview. Check that the blue-tiled recess is entirely inside Pool, then click Apply.

← PreviousNext: Fill the basin →

Pool · 22 / 34

Fill the basin

  1. Open Tools > → Environment / space > → Water volume.
  2. Enter X 21.5, Z 2, Width 3 and Depth 3. They must match the basin.
  3. Set World surface Y to -0.1 and World bottom Y to -0.4.
  4. Leave the remaining pages at their defaults. Click Preview, check the water is inside the recess, and click Apply.
  5. Save.

The deck is at height 0. The water surface sits 10 cm below it, and the bottom matches the basin’s 40 cm depth. That leaves about 30 cm of water.

These matching form values are useful here: the water and basin edges need to agree. Water doesn’t dig a hole by itself, which is why we built and checked the dry basin first.

Elevators · 26 / 34

Give each room an elevator cabin

The cabins sit behind the front walls. We’ll give each one a name, then connect them. All three rooms are on the same floor; the linked cabins let you travel between separate locations.

  1. Zoom out until you can see Backrooms again. Click its lobby floor and press F. Keep the overhead view and tilt it slightly if needed so you can point at the inside face of its front outside wall.
  2. Choose Build → Elevator. Point near the middle of that front wall, well away from the corners.
  3. Check the preview: the entrance faces the lobby and the cabin extends behind the wall. Click to place it.
  4. In the panel that opens, click Name, select all, type Backrooms, and press Return. Close the panel.
  5. Repeat on Home’s front outside wall. Name that cabin Home.
  6. Repeat on Pool’s front outside wall. Name that cabin Pool. Keep it clear of the basin and the bench.

Use the outside front wall, not the internal divider or closet wall. If the preview is on the wrong side, press Esc, tilt the view to see the inside face and start Elevator again. Don’t place it until the entrance faces the room.

Before you continue

You have exactly three cabins, named Backrooms, Home and Pool. Save.

← PreviousNext: Link the three stops →

Elevators · 27 / 34

Link the three stops

  1. Return your view to the Backrooms cabin. Choose Build → Elevator, then click the existing cabin to open its panel. Check that the selected name is Backrooms.
  2. Click Link selected. The panel closes so you can pick a destination in the scene.
  3. Pan or zoom over to Home and click its existing elevator cabin. The link is made in both directions, and the panel opens again.
  4. In the cabin list, click Backrooms to select it again, then click Link selected.
  5. Pan or zoom over to Pool and click its cabin.
  6. Check the panel lists all three named stops. Close it and save.

These links join the cabins into one group, so each cabin can reach the other two. You don’t need to build an extra Home–Pool link or create another elevator.

Before you continue

Each cabin offers the other two rooms as destinations. Save, close and reopen the project to confirm the links were kept.

Try and save · 28 / 34

Take your first walk

  1. Click Save, then Test in editor in the top bar, or press ⌘P / Ctrl P.
  2. Wait for the first-person view. You start in the Backrooms lobby.
  3. Use W, A, S, D to walk and the mouse to look around. Walk through the open doorway, around the pillars, and up to the desk and chair.
  4. Press E while looking at an interactive door or elevator control. Give swinging doors room to move before walking through.
  5. Press Esc when you’re ready to return to the editor.

Take this first walk slowly. You can reach everything in this space without jumping.

Test in editor uses a temporary snapshot and approximate, unbaked lighting. Your bright overhead editing view also uses an inspection light. Judge the finished lighting in the built player version below.

Try and save · 29 / 34

Build the finished lighting

  1. Stop the first-person test with Esc.
  2. Click Final build at the upper right. It saves changed source and prepares the selected level.
  3. Wait for the bottom status line to say Build succeeded.

The progress bar belongs to the current stage, so it can fill up and start again as the compiler moves on. That doesn’t mean it has restarted the entire job. Leave the editor running until it reports success.

If it reports an error, read the message and fix the problem before continuing. A previous successful package is not proof that your latest changes built.

Try and save · 30 / 34

Export a package you can play

  1. Click Project > → Export all levels + sources.
  2. Enter a full destination path ending in .placesmap, in an existing folder you can write to. For example, on Mac use /Users/your-name/Documents/first-space.placesmap; on Windows use C:/Users/your-name/Documents/first-space.placesmap. Replace your-name with your account folder.
  3. Click Apply and wait for the export to succeed. The panel closes while it works.

The exported .placesmap is the file you import into the game. Keep the original project folder too—it’s the easiest place to continue editing.

Final build prepares the selected level. Export all levels + sources creates the complete project package and includes editable sources. For this tutorial, the three rooms are all inside one level.

Before you continue

Your destination folder contains the exported .placesmap file.

Try and save · 31 / 34

Bring the package into Places

  1. Open the game: Places.app on Mac, or places.exe in the Windows Places folder. The Mac portable player executable is places.
  2. Choose Explore. A fresh installation may already show the empty Explore library.
  3. Choose Import folder. Finder or File Explorer opens the game’s import folder.
  4. Copy your exported first-space.placesmap into that folder. Keep your original export as well.
  5. Return to Places and choose Import levels.
  6. When My First Places Space appears, select its card to start playing.

Import levels reads the import folder; it isn’t a file-picker button. If it says no new maps were found, check that the exported .placesmap—not the editable project folder—is in the folder opened by Import folder.

Try and save · 32 / 34

Try the whole route

  1. Walk around Backrooms and through its open doorway. Check the desk, chair and three pillars leave a comfortable path.
  2. Return to the lobby. Open the elevator with its entrance control if necessary, then walk fully inside.
  3. Turn toward the button panel beside the cabin entrance. Aim at the buttons until the on-screen prompt reads Floor: Home, then use that control by pressing E or left-clicking. Step clear of the doorway and let the doors close and the ride finish.
  4. Leave the Home cabin, open the bedroom door with E, and walk inside. Look at the bed and kitchen. If the door swings toward you, step back, wait for it to open, then line up with the middle of the opening.
  5. Use Home’s elevator to visit Pool. Walk along the deck between the basin and bench, then turn toward the closet door. Open it and check that you can enter. Find the bucket, bench and duck.
  6. Walk down into the shallow pool and back onto the deck. You should not need to jump to get out.
  7. Ride back to Backrooms. Try the remaining return trips too.

The green door-arrow control opens or closes the doors at the current stop. To travel, aim at a destination button and read the floor name in the on-screen prompt before you use it. If the doors keep reopening, move farther inside the cabin so you’re clear of the doorway.

Before you continue

You can reach every room, use both doors, enter and leave the pool, and return to where you started.

Keep your work and make it yours

Save the editor project whenever you finish a section. To return later, launch the editor, click Open, and choose the project folder containing places.project.json. Don’t select the exported .placesmap in this folder chooser.

Back up the whole project folder, including its levels folder. If you make changes, repeat Final build and Export, then import the new package into the game before judging the finished result.

You now know the core workflow: create a floor and ceiling, draw walls, cut openings or fit doors, choose surfaces, place props and lights, connect cabins, then test and export. Try moving the furniture, changing a material, or adding another light. Save a copy of the project first if you want to keep this first version.

Before you continue

Keep the editable project and exported package in places you can find again.

When something doesn’t behave as expected

What you seeWhat to check
A tool seems stuck on the pointerPress Esc to cancel it, then choose the tool again.
The wrong object changes when you pick a propA prop was still selected. Undo, click clear floor, then choose the new model and Place chosen prop.
A prop won’t placeRead the bottom message. Pan until the center of the view is over clear floor, then try again. Large props need more room.
A drag selects the floor instead of moving the objectSelect the object again, wait for its colored handles, and drag a colored line. After Duplicate, use the new copy’s handles.
A room looks rooflessCeilings are hidden from above. Look up from inside the first-person test to see the ceiling.
A new Room has no wallsThat is expected. Draw its four walls with Build → Wall.
A wall won’t take a materialSelect a solid wall face, confirm Properties says Wall, then open its Material button. This resets the catalog’s surface target.
A door blocks you while openingStep back to give it room to swing, then walk through the center of the gap.
The water covers the deckCheck the water and basin share X, Z, width and depth. Water surface must be -0.1 and the bottom -0.4 for this example.
The game shows an older versionSave, rebuild, export again, copy the new package into Import folder and run Import levels.

Simple-mode limits: Room creation opens a form rather than drawing a rectangle. The pillar, recessed floor and water tools also expose forms. We use them where they make the required shape or matching basin/water values clear. The rest of this guide uses the visual tools available in Simple; no step requires switching to Advanced.
